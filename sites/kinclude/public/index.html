The bind

Their first phone shouldn't come with the whole internet's address book.

Mainstream messaging lets anyone find, add and message a child. So parents are left choosing between banning it altogether and reading everything in secret.

Neither builds trust. Kinclude is built for the third option: a small, closed room where the only people in it are people you both chose — and where how much you can see is agreed out in the open.

How it will work

No way in from outside.

  1. 1

    Nothing to find.

    No search. No usernames to look up. No friend requests. No strangers — because there's no door for them to knock on.

  2. 2

    In by invitation.

    Everyone joins by an invitation that you and your child both approve — grandparents, cousins, their best friend's family.

  3. 3

    Readable only by the room.

    Encrypted end to end: the only people who can read a conversation are the people in it — and, at the tier you've agreed, you. No account with us, no operator holding the guest list, no ads, nothing to sell.

Oversight, agreed together

Oversight that grows up with them.

Three tiers, each a little further back. You choose together where your family starts, and when to move on.

What's planned

In design

Everything a family chat needs. Nothing it doesn't.

  • Family rooms

    A room for the whole house, or one for just the cousins. Everyone in it is someone you both chose.

  • One-to-one chats

    Private conversations between two people in the family — still with nobody outside it.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can strangers find my child?

No. There's no search, no username to look up and no friend requests. The only way into a room is an invitation that you and your child both approved.

Will my teenager know if I read their messages?

Yes. At every tier above the first, they can see exactly what you've read, or that you opened their messages in an emergency. At the first tier they know you can see everything, because the chat says so.

What counts as an emergency?

You decide. What doesn't change is that your child is always told, straight away, and that notice can't be switched off.

Is it encrypted if I can read it?

Yes. Encrypted end to end means only the people in a conversation can read it. At the tier you've agreed, you're one of them: a member of what you're allowed to see, not a back door. Nobody outside the family, including us, can read anything.

Is it ready?

No. Kinclude is being designed now, and there's no date. This page describes how it's meant to work, not something you can install yet.

Who runs it?

Nobody holds your family's guest list. There's no account to register, and no operator keeping a record of who is in which room. It's made by ForgeSworn, who also make Kindred.
